# S2 · Downtown and junctions

Section: Mon Wharf ramp → Market & Fort Pitt → Market & Third → Market Square → Forbes & Smithfield → Smithfield &
Fort Pitt → Smithfield Street Bridge → Station Square (≈ 2.7–5.3 km). Shared system: **junctions** — real
intersection geometry for the city's street graph, route-agnostic, on for the whole Pittsburgh route.

## What changed

Before, every street was an independent ribbon: at a crossing two ribbons simply lay on top of each other, 2 cm
apart (height slots), with both streets' lane paint and both sidewalks running straight through, and the route's
pale shoulder band crossing every side street. Now:

- **One surface per junction** — a polygon built from the arms' mouths and rounded curb returns (radius 4.5 m on
  residential streets, 6 m on primary/secondary, 9 m on motorway links), plain asphalt, level side to side.
- **Street ribbons trimmed** to the junction edge (each arm at its curb return's tangent point), so no ribbon is drawn
  over another inside a junction. Their lift blends to the junction's over 6 m, so the surfaces meet without a step.
- **Sidewalks wrap the corners** (2.5 m, following each curb return; 2 m on the route's side, like its shoulder).
- **Paint**: a continental crosswalk across every arm with sidewalks (2–6 classes) of a junction of three or more arms,
  a stop line across the incoming lanes (both lanes on a one-way street coming in, none going out). No crosswalk where
  the block is too short for one or it would land in the next junction.
- **The route's own road** in a junction: plain asphalt (no edge or centre lines across the junction), its shoulder
  band cut at the junction's edge, and a crosswalk across the road at each mouth where a street with sidewalks comes in.
- **Merged OSM nodes**: nodes within 7 m (or 0.7 × the streets' widths) merge into one junction (dual carriageways,
  turn lanes), never wider than 34 m; links between merged nodes lie inside the junction and aren't drawn.
- Bridges, decks and ramps (`st.bridge`) are left alone, and so are streets meeting more than 1.2 m apart in height.

**Cost**: +1 draw call (the route overlay, one mesh for the whole route like the road); junction surfaces, paint and
corner sidewalks go into the existing street and sidewalk chunk meshes (the street texture grew a white block for the
paint: 128 × 256, lanes in its left half). +≈ 135 k triangles over the whole city (88 k street builders, 44 k
sidewalks, 3 k route overlay), +60–90 k in a typical downtown view; draw-call differences between before/after shots
are within the life simulation's noise (±10). Load time (Node, this shared 2-CPU box): planning ≈ 250 ms, meshes
≈ 200–350 ms; expect a third of that on the M4.

## How to use it (any city route)

Scene key: `"junctions": true` in the scene JSON (or an object overriding `DEFAULTS` in `js/world/junctions.js`, e.g.
`{ "mergeM": 9, "easeM": 14 }`). Off (no key): everything is exactly as before.

- `js/world/junctions.js` (pure, no three.js — the course QC and the tests use it):
  - `planJunctions(streets, ctx, options)` → `{ list, cuts, spans, options }`. Called once from `cityStreets()` when the
    landscape has `junctions` set (`landscape._junctions`). Each junction: `{ id, x, z, route, s, arms, extra, poly,
    curbs, J(x, z), lift }` — `poly` the surface outline (counter-clockwise in x/z), `curbs` the curb returns (for corner
    furniture), `arms[]` `{ k (street, −1 = the route), u (direction), h (half width), t (trim from its node), a0, dir,
    cls, oneway }`, `J(x, z)` the surface's height line (drawn at `+ lift`). `cuts`: street index → arc-length ranges not
    drawn. `spans`: `{ jn, s0, s1 }` route distances the route's road passes through a junction.
  - `streetPieces(st, cuts)` — a street's drawn pieces; `streetPoint(st, s)`; `streetOverlaps(streets, plan, ctx)` — the
    QC count; `drawnLiftMax(p, plan, maxLift)`.
- `js/world/junction-mesh.js` (three.js): `buildJunctionMeshes(city, plan, { get, roads, walks, liftOf })` (called by
  `CityScene._streetsAndLamps`), `drawnPieces(st, k, plan, lift)`, `junctionPlan(landscape, index, route)`,
  `cutRouteRibbon(mesh, plan, dist)` and `routeJunctionOverlay(route, index, plan)` (called by `world.js`).
- Street points carry `p.jn` (inside junction `jn`: not drawn, height = the junction's) and `p.je` / `p.jd` (in the ease
  zone of junction `je`, `jd` metres past its edge).

### Heights: the hand-off to road alignment (S4)

- **S4 owns street heights and centrelines between junctions; junctions own them inside.** Order inside
  `cityStreets()`: the streets' own profile (ground, decks, the keep-under-the-route clamp — and S4's smoothing, which
  must run *before* the `[junctions]` hook) → `planJunctions` sets every point within an arm's trim distance to the
  junction surface (`p.jn`) and eases the next `max(easeM = 10 m, step / easeGrade = 5 %)` (at most half-way to the next
  junction) back to the street's own profile (`p.je`, `p.jd`).
- A junction the route passes through follows **the route's height** (level across, along its grade), 3 cm under its
  road surface; the route's heights (`route.ele`) are never changed. A street-only junction is a least-squares plane
  through its arms (≤ 10 % slope), pushed under the route's road where it reaches it.
- S4's street step / grade checks should skip `p.jn` points and allow the ease zone up to `easeGrade`.
- **Plan smoothing must keep shared OSM nodes shared**: a node used by two streets has to stay at bit-identical
  coordinates in both (junctions are found by exact coordinates; `st.nodes` / `st.nodeIdx` map them into `st.pts`), and
  should not move more than ~1 m (the curb returns are fitted to the arms' directions at their nodes).

### QC

- Course QC (`npm run qc`) prints, for a city route, `streets: X km of ribbon over other streets, Y km under the
  route's road, Z m into other junctions; N junctions (M on the route)` — with or without junctions, so before/after
  is one scene key apart. New fail check `junction-over-road` (a junction surface reaching the road's surface);
  `street-over-road` now skips points inside junctions (not drawn) and uses the blended lift near a cut.
- `test/junctions.test.mjs`: a synthetic crossing and T onto a route, and the Pittsburgh plan (overlap < 12 km, every
  junction under the road).

## For the other sections

- **S1 (baked lighting)**: junction surfaces, paint and corner sidewalks live in the `Streets <key>` / `Sidewalks <key>`
  meshes (bake them like the streets); the route overlay `Route junctions` (world.js) sits 4–8 mm over the road — treat
  it like the road. The street texture is now 128 wide (u 0–.5 lanes, .5–1 paint).
- **S3 (detail kit)**: crosswalks and stop lines are drawn here (arm `t` + 0.5 … `t` + 3.5 m, stop line 1.1 m behind);
  keep furniture off `jn.poly` and the crosswalks. Signal poles, push buttons, hydrants and bollards belong at the curb
  returns: `jn.curbs[].pts` (the curb line; the sidewalk lies outward from the junction, `wA`/`wB` wide), e.g. a signal
  at each return's middle 0.6 m outward for junctions with ≥ 3 arms of class ≤ 4. If S3 raises sidewalks onto a curb,
  set `sidewalkLift` to match. **Market Square** is still the bare beige plaza land colour: it needs plaza paving and
  furniture from the kit (and the builder only exports the land raster — 12 m cells — so real plaza outlines would need
  `build_city.py` to export pedestrian areas).
- **S4 (road alignment)**: the hand-off above, plus two route-centreline problems in this section that junctions can't
  fix: (1) 3.3–3.5 km round Market Square — the corner rounding (±12 m windows, then the tight-spot passes) turns three
  grid corners 30–60 m apart into one long arc through the block corners, so the road zig-zags across the mapped
  streets and four junctions crowd the arc; (2) 4.55–4.65 km, off the Smithfield Street Bridge onto Carson Street — the
  route cuts the corner 27 m from the real intersection (Carson & Smithfield), so that junction is a street-only one
  beside the road and the route meets Carson in a second, made-up junction.

## What's left

  ramps merging at grade, service roads) — a second pass could treat "ribbons within a width of each other at the same
  level" as virtual junctions or merge them into one wider carriageway.
- The route's road still runs over the leftover street it rides on (7.8 km under the route; hidden by the road but it
  peeks out where the route is smoothed off the mapped line).
- Turn-lane arrows, lane lines on wide arms, raised curbs (with S3), plaza paving (Market Square, PPG Place).
- Junction meshes have no distance LOD (they ride in the street chunks); paint is ~57 k triangles city-wide.
